Integrations

Integrations are chosen when you install. Tick them in the app, or pass them as options to the unfr0zen command-line tool: on Linux and macOS, the app's own executable run with arguments. Windows has the app only. To add or remove one later, run the install again with the new selection. MQTT, Matter, Zigbee and Doom are served by nestlocal, which any of them installs on the thermostat.

On Linux, run both the app and CLI as your normal user, without sudo. Both use the same one-time USB permission setup, which requires an administrator; neither frontend requests elevated privileges. Both save run logs under ~/.local/share/unfr0zen/logs/, or $XDG_DATA_HOME/unfr0zen/logs/ when set.

MQTT and Matter reach your network over Wi-Fi: join it from the thermostat's own menu (Settings → Network). That network doesn't need internet access.

The 3rd and 2nd generation are supported. The 1st generation is experimental and untested on a unit: its radio uses UART, so it has no Zigbee or Matter over Thread, and it has no USB shell. The Nest Thermostat E is detected and refused.

To build your own integration, the MQTT topics, Zigbee clusters and Matter endpoints are documented under Protocols.

UNFR0ZEN menu

The install replaces the thermostat's NEST APP settings entry with UNFR0ZEN. It shows Matter pairing, Zigbee pairing with a 30-second countdown, and Doom. An integration's entry appears only when it was installed.

The original nlclient is saved once on the unit, at /etc/unfr0zen/nlclient.original, and kept across later installs. No prebuilt binary patches are used: the app links the menu into the unit's own nlclient. Supported versions and undo: platform/common/nlclient/README.md.

MQTT

AppCLI
Relay to MQTT broker, Broker host[:port]--mqtt host[:port]
User, Password--mqtt-user, --mqtt-password
TLS, CA certificate--mqtt-tls, --mqtt-ca FILE

The thermostat connects over Wi-Fi directly to your MQTT broker (for example the Mosquitto add-on), and Home Assistant's MQTT integration discovers and controls it there. No Nest cloud, external relay or separate thermostat bridge is involved; the MQTT broker is the only intermediary. The port defaults to 1883, or 8883 with TLS. The other MQTT options need the broker, a password needs a user, and a CA certificate turns on TLS. The thermostat shows up in Home Assistant through MQTT discovery: a climate entity with off, heat, cool, heat-cool, eco and fan modes, setpoints and range, what it is running, eco and the eco temperatures, humidity and target humidity, outdoor temperature, occupancy, °C/°F, backplate temperature, battery, fan and its timer, Auto-Schedule, Home/Away Assist, Sunblock, Early-On, the eco heat and cool limits, the indicators (Leaf, Early-On and Sunblock active, safety shutoff, filter), time to temperature, filter runtime, IP address, Wi-Fi signal and clearing the schedule; with the equipment, hot water, humidifier and dehumidifier state and humidity control.

Two Home Assistant blueprints feed the thermostat what it used to get from Google. Both need the MQTT relay; on Zigbee alone, see Zigbee.

Weather. Sends the outdoor temperature and humidity, conditions and forecast, every 15 minutes and as soon as the weather entity comes back after being unavailable. Import it, then create an automation from it and pick your thermostat and a weather entity. Outdoor sensors are optional: without them it sends the weather entity's temperature and humidity. Temperatures in °F are converted to °C.

Clock. The thermostat sets its clock from an NTP server; on a network with no way out to one, this blueprint has Home Assistant answer its time requests instead. Import it, then create an automation from it. It has no settings and answers every thermostat.

Matter

AppCLI
Matter--matter
Pairing code--matter-code CODE

Works alone or beside MQTT. The thermostat must already be on Wi-Fi. It is commissioned with its 11-digit pairing code, 0347-100-5534 unless you set another. A code of your own needs a valid check digit, and --matter-code turns on Matter. The first pairing window opens by itself: UNFR0ZEN → Matter shows Ready to pair with the QR and pairing code. When pairing completes, the screen automatically replaces the codes with Paired and Enable pairing. To pair again, choose Enable pairing → Forget and pair. The confirmation warns that this immediately forgets all Matter controllers before opening a new pairing window. This cannot be undone, even if the new pairing fails; Wi-Fi and Zigbee settings are retained. Choose Back to cancel without changing the current pairing.

  1. Home Assistant: Settings → Devices & services → Add integration → Matter.
  2. Add the device with the pairing code, or scan the QR shown on the thermostat.
nestlocal uses the Matter test vendor ID (0xFFF1) and test attestation, so controllers treat it as uncertified. Home Assistant's Matter server accepts it only with its test-net DCL option on.

Home Assistant shows the thermostat (modes, setpoints and range, what it is running, eco as a preset, humidity, outdoor temperature), occupancy, °C/°F, backplate temperature, battery, fan and its timer, and switches for Auto-Schedule, Home/Away Assist, Sunblock, Early-On, the eco limits and, with a humidifier or dehumidifier, humidity control. The other MQTT entities aren't offered over Matter.

Bluetooth commissioning. On 3rd generation units with the supported DA14580 Bluetooth firmware, Matter also commissions over Bluetooth, automatically. The stock radio image stays unchanged: nestlocal loads the unit's own firmware into radio RAM. Bluetooth advertises only while the pairing window is open; day-to-day traffic goes over Wi-Fi or Thread. Units with missing or unsupported Bluetooth firmware pair over IP.

Sleep. The thermostat sleeps between Matter commands: unicast UDP to port 5540 wakes it. It stays awake while commissioning, and on firmware that rejects the wake filters. Multicast discovery and subscription deadlines don't wake it yet. This is experimental. On the 2nd generation, Matter keeps the unit awake: its sleep daemon wakes only for TCP traffic.

Matter over Thread

AppCLI
Thread support (replaces Heat Link), under Matter--matter-thread

The thermostat joins a Thread mesh as a router and serves Matter over it, beside Wi-Fi. The commissioner supplies the Thread credentials. It needs Matter and takes over the radio from Nest's Thread firmware (Heat Link), so it can't be combined with Zigbee. Experimental. A 2nd generation unit's radio holds one firmware at a time: nestlocal flashes a Thread image for it, and keeps the unit awake while Thread runs. Design and device details: platform/common/radio/thread/README.md.

Zigbee

AppCLI
Zigbee (replaces Heat Link)--zigbee

The thermostat joins your Zigbee network as a router through its own 802.15.4 radio, alone or beside MQTT and Matter. On first start nestlocal writes its own radio firmware in place of Nest's Thread firmware (Heat Link). Installing again without Zigbee puts Nest's firmware back. Both generations use Nest's -15 dBm Zigbee transmit setting.

A 3rd generation Heat Link model (Display-3.x, Display-5.x) on firmware 6.4-8 can stop on error H71 or H74 once the Heat Link can no longer connect. With Zigbee or Thread, the Works With Nest boiler option (--wwn-boiler) sets such a unit up as heating a Works With Nest boiler, so it keeps heating through W1 and its safety temperature, runs on its real backplate's sensors, and Zigbee, Matter and MQTT offer heat. It stays off unless chosen. Installing again without it takes the setting back. While it is set: Safety Temp shows a notice instead of its values, which stay in force; Equipment's Heating Equipment item does nothing, as the boiler's pages need Nest's cloud; Pro Setup adds the boiler's supply and hot water pages, whose values go nowhere; and with nothing on W1 the thermostat offers no heat mode of its own, so the boiler has to be driven from the setpoint, as with a relay from Home Assistant.

Any Zigbee 3.0 coordinator works: the thermostat is controlled through standard clusters, so ZHA needs no converter or quirk, though the thermostat's switches need Home Assistant with zha 0.0.61 (June 2025) or later. Zigbee2MQTT is recommended, with the converter that names every entity and carries the weather:

  1. Zigbee2MQTT 2.12.1 or later: copy nest-learning-thermostat.mjs to data/external_converters/ and restart Zigbee2MQTT.
  2. Permit join on the coordinator. The thermostat joins the strongest network permitting joins, on any channel, and keeps it across restarts.
  3. After updating the converter, re-interview the device to refresh its generation-specific model, then Reconfigure it so Home Assistant gets the new entities.

Installing this version moves the thermostat's switches from the On/Off cluster to Binary Output, so ZHA names them. A thermostat already paired has to be paired again, in ZHA or, with the converter updated, re-interviewed in Zigbee2MQTT. Delete the old switch entities that stay behind.

With the converter, Home Assistant gets the MQTT entities under the same names, except the IP address, Wi-Fi signal and the separate Temperature and HVAC action sensors, which are on its climate entity. The thermostat sets its clock from the coordinator (Zigbee2MQTT or ZHA answer its Time cluster reads by themselves), so the clock blueprint is not needed. The weather has a blueprint of its own for Zigbee2MQTT, which sends it through the converter: import it, then create an automation from it and pick your thermostat, Zigbee2MQTT base topic (default zigbee2mqtt) and a weather entity. Outdoor sensors are optional: without them it sends the weather entity's temperature and humidity. Temperatures in °F are converted to °C. Nothing else needs Wi-Fi.

A router needs its own trust-centre link key to help other devices join a coordinator that uses per-device keys. If it still has only the global joining key, enable pairing again on that thermostat to obtain its individual key.

To move to another network, open UNFR0ZEN → Zigbee → Enable pairing and confirm. The thermostat beeps, leaves the network, forgets its credentials and looks for one permitting joins for 30 seconds. After a timeout it stays off any network until pairing is enabled again or nestlocal restarts.

The radio keeps routing while the thermostat sleeps, and its interrupt wakes the unit for a message. The radio-wake module supports the 2nd generation's kernel and the kernels of the published 3rd-generation releases (5.8 to 6.1). Without a matching module, messages wait until the unit wakes for something else. The 2nd generation's native client prevents sleep while a USB cable is connected.

Doom

AppCLI
Doom, under Patch → Advanced--doom

Installs Doom with the shareware episode (DOOM1.WAD v1.9, included). Start it from UNFR0ZEN → Doom.

  • You walk forward all along. Turn the ring to steer, press it to fire. Doors and switches open as you walk into them.
  • In menus, the ring moves and a press selects.
  • Hold the ring for 3 seconds to quit back to the thermostat.
  • Sound comes from the piezo: the PC speaker effects and each song's highest note.
While Doom runs the thermostat does not control the heating or cooling.

Backup & restore

AppCLI
Back up unitunfr0zen backup [OUT]
Not availableunfr0zen verify IMAGE
Restore to unit, Restore from fileunfr0zen restore IMAGE

Backs up the whole NAND as an image, with a .sha256 beside it: 512 MiB ECC-corrected and checked with a second read on the 3rd generation; raw pages with their OOB on the 1st and 2nd, where a block the ECC doesn't find clean is read until two reads agree. Nothing is written to the NAND during a backup. To resume an interrupted backup, run unfr0zen backup OUT again with the same OUT; the app and a run without OUT start a new file. Restore needs the .sha256, writes the verified image back and asks first. On the 1st and 2nd generation, the image's factory-bad blocks must match the chip's, only blocks that differ are rewritten, and an interrupted restore is run again.
